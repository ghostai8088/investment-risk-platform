## Part 0 — What RM-1 actually is

PM-1 (ENT-053, migration `0031`) persists a **governed dated per-period time-weighted return series** — `n` `DIETZ_PERIOD` sub-period rows plus one `TWR_LINKED` summary per COMPLETED run, each binding `calculation_run` + `dataset_snapshot` + a registered `model_version`, immutable append-only. Nothing consumes it for *risk-of-the-return-series* statistics. RM-1 is that consumer: the trailing-window statistics a supervisor reads next to a return.

It is deliberately **not** Sharpe (SR-1, ratified next) and not a re-litigation of PM-1's scope: PM-1's registered assumptions already name √T/^T scaling as "a later declared transform" (`perf/bootstrap.py:143-146`), and RM-1 is that slice.

### 2.1 Annualization, per metric, with the provisions stated in full
- **GIPS 2.A.12 [V]**, verbatim: *"Returns for periods of less than one year must not be annualized."* Restated at 8.A.4 for advertisements. A hard `MUST NOT`, binding on **returns** only.
- **GIPS 4.A.1.j [V]**, with its three conditions restored (the draft dropped them): *"**For composites for which monthly composite returns are available**, the three-year annualized ex post standard deviation (using monthly returns) of the composite **and the benchmark** **as of each annual period end**"*, fn 32: *"Required for periods ending on or after 1 January 2011."* So it is **conditional and point-in-time-per-annual-period** — it authorises the ×√12 operator and the monthly input, **not** a continuous rolling emission. Emitting at every period end is OUR superset. **4.B.3/4.B.4/4.B.5 are recommendations ("should"), not requirements.**
- **The operator is ×√12 [V][C]** — corroborated by reproducing CFA Institute's four published 3-year figures from the underlying 36 monthly returns to 16 significant digits, which also establishes **arithmetic-mean centring** and that **GIPS does not prescribe n vs n−1**.
- **Return annualization operator [V]:** `R_ann = (1+R)^(12/window_months) − 1` (GIPS's geometric convention). Note at `window_months = 12` the exponent is exactly 1, so the annualized return **is definitionally the cumulative return** (OD-RM-1-G).
- **Maximum drawdown: never annualized.** The draft rested this on Magdon-Ismail's log/√T/linear asymptotics; the verifier showed those describe the **unbounded arithmetic** drawdown `D̄`, whereas RM-1 emits the **bounded fraction of peak** `f ∈ [0,1]`, whose growth in T necessarily saturates — so the trichotomy does not transfer. **The conclusion survives and is stronger**: a bounded, saturating, horizon-monotone statistic has *no* horizon-scaling law at all, which needs no citation. MDD is also outside 2.A.12's scope (not a rate).
- **k = 12 is cited [V]; k = 252/52/365 is NOT [U].** RM-1's grid is monthly, so √12 is the only basis needed and `QS-14` is satisfied by citation.

### 2.2 The two choices the standard declines to make
- **n vs n−1 [V][C]:** GIPS does not prescribe it. Materiality **at the window RM-1 actually ships** (the draft quoted only n=36 and understated by 3×): `√(n/(n−1))−1` = **+4.45% at n=12**, +1.42% at n=36.
- **"Unbiased" is the wrong word for σ [C]:** n−1 gives an unbiased **variance**; its square root is **biased low** by `1−c4(n)` ≈ **2.24% at n=12** — comparable to the choice above. GIPS and CFA Institute both use the square root of the n−1 variance and neither applies a c4 correction; RM-1 follows, and says so precisely.
- **Arithmetic-mean centring despite geometric linking [V][C]** — an internal tension in the standard, documented rather than silently resolved.

### 2.3 The crux: irregular sub-periods are the wrong sample — re-grounded on the standard
**Primary argument [V] (standards):** GIPS defines the risk statistic **only** on the monthly series (4.A.1.j, with its conditions) and requires the input to be valued *"At least monthly"* and *"**As of the calendar month end or the last business day of the month**"* (2.A.23.a/b — the draft truncated clause (b), which matters: see OD-RM-1-F), with sub-period returns geometrically linked up to that grid (2.A.24.f). Sub-period returns are, by construction, **inputs only** — the Guidance Statement defines the sub-period as "the period between external cash flows".

**Corroborating intuition [V] (estimator theory), explicitly demoted:** Lo Eq. (19) — `Var[Rₜ(q)] = qσ² + 2σ² Σ_{k=1}^{q−1} (q−k)ρₖ` (summation limits restored; Lo stresses "the **first q − 1** autocorrelations"). At ρ=0 this is `qσ²`, so variance scales with interval length and unequal sub-periods are heteroskedastic. Three honest caveats the draft lacked: (i) Eq. (16) defines the q-period return as a **sum** — "where I have ignored the effects of compounding **for computational convenience**" (body text at Eq. 16, n. 7; the draft misattributed this to fn 7 alone and dropped the purpose clause), and n. 7 in fact identifies the exact fix (*continuously compounded* returns) which RM-1 does not adopt; (ii) applying Eq. (19) to a Dietz series posits a latent daily stationary process, an unstated assumption; (iii) the ρ=0 step is an i.i.d. assumption the platform's own desmoothing slices exist because it is false for its books.
**And the honest limit of the fix [C]:** calendar months are 28–31 days, so the monthly grid is *itself* heteroskedastic by ~5.2% (≈7.6% on trading days). The monthly grid is **conventionalized by the standard, not made homogeneous by mathematics** — a difference of degree, not kind. RM-1 therefore refutes fork option (a) on the **standards** ground, not on a claim that its own grid is homogeneous.

*(The draft additionally used GIPS 2.A.18.a as a third forcing leg. That provision is scoped *"When calculating additional risk measures"* against a benchmark, and RM-1 v1 has no benchmark leg — so it is **void for v1** and has been moved to OD-RM-1-N as the binding constraint on the v2 benchmark leg, where it is genuinely load-bearing.)*

### 2.4 Maximum drawdown: clean definitions, zero standards coverage, and a citation scope limit
- **"Drawdown" appears twice in GIPS 2020, both meaning capital calls [V].** MDD is an **"additional risk measure"**, governed by 2.A.18 (*when* calculated for benchmark comparison), **4.C.43.a** (*"If the firm presents additional risk measures, the firm must: a. Describe any additional risk measure"* — conditional and sub-parted; the draft quoted it unconditionally) and **4.C.44** (*must disclose gross- vs net-of-fees for presented risk measures*).
- **Structure [V]:** Magdon-Ismail Eq. (1) `D̄(T) = sup_{t}[ sup_{s≤t} X(s) − X(t) ]` — a **level path**, **running peak, no look-ahead**. Chekhlov Def. 3.1/3.2 give the discrete form `ξₖ = max_{j≤k} wⱼ − wₖ`, `MaxDD = max_k ξₖ`; Prop. 3.4 places `MaxDD`/`AvDD` as the α-CDD endpoints. Chekhlov sets `w_0 = 0` and notes ξ_0 is always zero — i.e. **the base point IS an observation** with drawdown 0 (load-bearing: OD-RM-1-H).
- **Scope limit the draft over-claimed [V]:** Chekhlov Prop. 3.1's non-negativity is proved for **AD on the uncompounded** cumulative return, and the paper explicitly says of the relative form *"DD does not satisfies the properties which AD does"* (sic). RM-1's measure is the **relative form on a compounded base** — the case the source disclaims. Non-negativity and boundedness are therefore **RM-1's own asserted invariants**, provable only under OD-RM-1-H's precondition.
- **Base [V]:** Chekhlov applies AD to the **uncompounded** sum for LP tractability (*"applied not to the compounded cumulative portfolio rate of return"*, §3 prose). RM-1 deliberately rejects that base for a compounded wealth index.
- **The ratio normalisation is OURS [U], and the draft's caveat was mathematically backwards.** With `X = log V`, `D = −log(1−f)`, so `f = 1 − e^{−D}` — a nonlinear transform, equal to the difference form only to first order in small `f`, **never exactly**. (The draft claimed exact equality "when X is a log level".) The paper is also unit-agnostic — plain arithmetic Brownian motion; "log" does not appear. Recorded with the Jensen gap noted (`E[1−e^{−D̄}] ≠ 1−e^{−E[D̄]}`) so nobody later reads the paper's `E[D̄]` as an expected percentage drawdown.
- **A citable discretisation bias [C]:** `sup(subset) ≤ sup(superset)`, so monthly MDD ≤ daily MDD ≤ continuous MDD **always**. Label the frequency; never compare across frequencies.
- **TWR index, not NAV [reasoned]:** a NAV path includes external flows, so a redemption would register as a "drawdown" that is not a performance event; the linked TWR index is flow-neutral by construction (2.A.24.f [V]).

### 2.5 Rolling-window conventions
- **"Rolling" appears zero times in GIPS 2020 [V].** Its 3-year measure is nonetheless the canonical convention: a **trailing, complete** window, evaluated *"as of each annual period end"*.
- **Incomplete windows are suppressed and the suppression disclosed [V]:** **4.C.36**, with its gate restored — *"**For composites with at least three annual periods of performance**, the firm must disclose if the three-year annualized ex post standard deviation of the composite **and/or benchmark** is not presented because 36 monthly returns are not available."* RM-1's suppression row for a 36-month window on a 19-month book is therefore emitted where GIPS mandates **nothing** — a good design choice, recorded as OUR convention rather than an obligation.
- **SEC §275.206(4)-1(d)(2) [V]** mandates 1/5/10-year trailing periods with equal prominence, ending no less recently than the most recent calendar year-end — an **anti-cherry-picking** provision, drafted as a prohibition ("unless"). Two carve-outs the draft omitted and which matter here: the **private-fund exclusion** (*"in each case other than any private fund"* — directly relevant given PPF-1/2/3) and the life-of-portfolio substitution. Whether an RM-1 read is an "advertisement" is a legal determination **outside this record** (OD-RM-1-N).

### OD-RM-1-F — **THE GRID: relink to calendar months; the alignment test is a THREE-condition conjunction**
The run relinks the pinned `DIETZ_PERIOD` sub-periods within each calendar month (via `link_periods`) and computes every statistic on the resulting **monthly** series.

**The criterion, corrected (V1-B1/B2 — the draft's version was vacuous and admitted the defect it exists to prevent).** Over the ordered boundary dates `d_0 … d_n`, **all three** must hold, and a failure is a **pre-create REFUSAL naming the offending boundary**:
1. `d_0` is a month-end;
2. `d_n` is a month-end;
3. every month-end strictly inside `(d_0, d_n)` appears among `d_1 … d_{n−1}`.

Conditions (1) and (2) are what make the span *partitionable*; (3) alone is not. The draft stated only (3), which (a) is **vacuously true for any series contained inside one month** — the campaign's `2026-05-18 → 05-26` span contains no month-end, so it passed, contradicting the draft's own text (§0.1) — and (b) admits a **partial leading or trailing month**: `2026-01-15, 01-31, 02-28` passes (3) while pooling a 16-day with a 28-day observation, whose standard deviations differ by 1.32× — precisely the heteroskedastic pooling §2.3 calls a governance defect. A partial first/last month is therefore a **refusal, not a truncation** (truncating would silently change the caller's requested span; imputing a valuation is prohibited). The campaign fixture is pinned as a reachability test so this claim can never rot.

**"Month-end" means the calendar month end OR the last business day of that month (V1-M2).** GIPS 2.A.23.b says *"As of the calendar month end **or the last business day of the month**"* — the draft truncated it, and the truncation matters: **2026-01-31 is a Saturday and 2026-05-31 a Sunday** (verified), so a firm valuing on the preceding Friday is fully compliant and a strict calendar-month-end gate would **refuse a GIPS-conforming book** while citing GIPS as authority. v1 implements the business-day allowance as the weaker, holiday-calendar-free rule: *exactly one boundary falls in each calendar month of the span and it is the last boundary in that month; `d_0` is the last boundary of the month preceding the first measured month.* A full holiday-aware convention is a recorded v2.

### OD-RM-1-G — Annualization, per metric
- **Rolling volatility: ANNUALIZED ×√12**, with the unannualized monthly σ emitted alongside so the transform is auditable. **The order is declared (V1-M13):** `σ_ann = quantize_HALF_UP(σ_monthly_stored × √12)` — computed from the **stored 12dp** value, so the emitted pair reconciles **exactly by construction**. This costs ≤2 ulp at 12dp versus a single-quantization path, accepted because it is what makes the two-row emission honest; a test asserts the exact reconciliation for every pair.
- **Rolling return: `R_ann = (1+R)^(12/window_months) − 1`** (§2.1), never below a 12-month window. **A 12-month window IS one year** (verifier-held: twelve consecutive month-ends span exactly 365/366 days), so expressing GIPS 2.A.12's threshold in months is coherent. **Honest relabelling (V1-M4):** under OQ-RM-1-4's `{12, 36}` parameter domain no governed caller can present `window_months < 12`, so the kernel guard is **defense-in-depth, not the enforcement point** — 2.A.12 compliance rests on the **registered parameter domain**, which is where it is actually enforced. Calling the guard "the invariant" would be a vacuous control. **At `window_months = 12` the annualized return is definitionally equal to the cumulative return**, so v1 suppresses the redundant annualized row at that window rather than shipping two always-identical governed numbers.
- **MDD: never annualized** (§2.1).
This discharges the annualization deferral recorded in four places — **explicitly, partially and narrowly** (volatility only, monthly grid, ×√12), in the dated-amendment form. **Where the discharge must NOT be written (V2-M4, verified):** PM-1's clause lives in the registered `PORTFOLIO_RETURN_ASSUMPTIONS` tuple (`perf/bootstrap.py:143-146`), and `resolve_or_register_version` returns an existing version untouched on a SELECT hit (`model/service.py:420-452`), so editing it would leave tenants carrying **different assumption text under the same `v1` label** — silent, un-audited divergence. PM-1's tuple is untouchable; the discharge lives in RM-1's own `model_assumption` rows plus the doc amendments.

### OD-RM-1-H — Maximum drawdown: fully determined, with a precondition
**A precondition, because the measure is otherwise undefined on a reachable input (V1-B3).** Every monthly return in the consumed series must satisfy **`1 + mⱼ > 0`**, enforced **pre-create** with a refusal naming the offending month. Without it: `EMV = 0` is legal in PM-1 and yields `r = −1` (verified), the wealth index hits zero and is **absorbing**; a month below −100% (reachable via PM-1's recorded no-cash-ledger limitation) makes the ratio-to-peak **exceed 1** (a "150% drawdown"), can **invert the sign of the response** (a −50% month making the reported drawdown *smaller*), and makes `(1+R)^(12/n)` raise `InvalidOperation` — an uncaught 500. This is **not** the magnitude gate: `r = −1` sits well inside `_MAX_RESULT_ABS`. A total-loss book therefore cannot carry a governed drawdown, recorded as a first-class limitation rather than allowed to become a 500.

**The construction, both previously-unspecified choices now declared (V1-B4):**
- **Window-local rebasing.** `V` is rebased to `1` at each window's opening boundary; the peak is `max` over observations **within that window only**. Run-global peaks would import a high-water mark from outside the window and report a "12-month maximum drawdown" for a drawdown that did not occur in it. Window-local is the only choice consistent with §2.4's "values over different windows are not comparable" and it makes **`MDD_36 ≥ MDD_12` at a common end date** hold — pinned as the test that proves this was resolved correctly.
- **The base point is an observation.** `V_0 = 1` at the window open, with `DD_0 = 0` (Chekhlov's `w_0`/`ξ_0` treatment). Omitting it makes a window that opens on a loss report **zero**: for `−10%` then eleven `+1%` months, including `V_0` gives `MDD = 0.10`, omitting it gives `0.00` — a 10pp error that converts a real drawdown into a value indistinguishable from "no drawdown".
- Running peak, no look-ahead; reported as `max_k[(peakₖ − Vₖ)/peakₖ]`, a **non-negative fraction ≤ 1** — asserted as RM-1's own kernel **post-condition** `0 ≤ MDD ≤ 1` (provable only under the precondition above; Chekhlov's Prop. 3.1 does not cover the relative-on-compounded form, §2.4).
- Every row carries its window and its sampling frequency (monthly), the statistic being horizon- and frequency-dependent and downward-biased by discretisation.

### OD-RM-1-I — Suppression: a first-class governed state, **nullable value + explicit flag**
An incomplete window emits a governed **suppressed** row. Granularity: **one row per `(metric_type, window_months)` per run**, not per evaluation point (per-point would collide on the grain at n=12 — V2-B1 — and is the worse reading of 4.C.36).

**Encoding, corrected (V1-M11).** Every sibling family has a NOT-NULL value column, which would force a stuffed `0.000000000000` — and **`0` is a legitimate governed value** for all three metrics (a monotonically rising window has `MDD = 0`; identical months give σ = 0). A suppressed row and a genuine zero would be **indistinguishable** on the read surface, and a naive consumer would read "not computable" as "no drawdown, excellent". The family's own doctrine forbids it: *"a stuffed placeholder would be dishonest provenance"* (`perf/models.py:258-261`). So: **nullable `metric_value` + NOT NULL `suppressed` boolean + `suppression_reason` (NULL iff not suppressed) + a DB `CHECK (suppressed = (metric_value IS NULL))`** — the DS-2/`0042` CHECK precedent. Recorded as a deliberate **departure from** the family's NOT-NULL column, with the 0028 rationale as the reason.

### OD-RM-1-J — A **four-column** grain
**`(calculation_run_id, metric_type, window_months, period_start)`**, `window_months` **NOT NULL**.

The sibling three-column grain is **insufficient** (both verifiers, independently; hand-verified at `perf/models.py:66-71`, `:138-143`): a two-window emission collides under *every* `period_start` convention — window-start collides for every `k ≤ n−36`, evaluation-date collides at every shared point — producing an `IntegrityError` at flush, i.e. a 500 inside the emit path, not a governed refusal.
