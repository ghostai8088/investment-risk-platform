## Part 0 — What SCH-2 is

RM-1 (ratified) computes on a **calendar-month grid** and refuses a series that is not month-aligned. That requires **exposure runs struck at month-ends**, and nothing can produce them: `SCHEDULABLE_RUN_TYPES = {VAR}` (`scheduling/events.py:49`), `CADENCE_KINDS = {INTERVAL}` (`:32`), and an interval grid cannot land on months of 28–31 days. SCH-2 therefore adds a month-end cadence, admits `EXPOSURE`, and replaces `dispatch_one`'s hardcoded `run_var` body with a **family dispatch registry** — which **discharges the SCH-1 deferral the Wave-11 close parked** for "whichever slice first ships family 2". Recorded debt paid, not new scope — but genuinely M/L-sized.

## Part 1 — The scheduler as it exists (`3211904`, hand-verified)

`Schedule` (ENT-061, EV, `scheduling/models.py:52-88`) — NOT append-only, symmetric FORCE RLS, no ops grant. `target_run_type` String(100) NOT NULL (`:67`); `scope_portfolio_id` NOT NULL (`:70-72`) — **family-neutral, fits EXPOSURE**; `model_version_id` NOT NULL (`:74-76`) — **blocks EXPOSURE**; `cadence_kind` String(20) NOT NULL (`:81`) — **zero test coverage today**; `interval_days` NOT NULL (`:83`) — INTERVAL-only; `anchor_date` NOT NULL (`:85`).

`ScheduledRun` (ENT-062, **IA append-only**, `:91-124`) — `uq(schedule_id, scheduled_for)` (`:104`) is the idempotency backstop; `resolved_exposure_run_id`/`resolved_covariance_run_id` (`:119-120`) are VaR-named and already nullable.

The grid: `current_tick` (`service.py:77-93`) with the **`if k < 0: k = 0` clamp at `:91-92`**; `_assert_current_tick` (`:96-106`) is the INV-SCH-1 write-boundary guard for both `dispatch_one:182` and `record_failed_dispatch:248`; `is_due` (`:109-120`); `select_active_due` (`:124-160`). **`interval_days` is read at exactly three sites** (`:104`, `:119`, `:151`), all calling `current_tick`.

`dispatch_one` (`:164-233`) is VaR-specific five ways: module-global imports (`:29-32`); a scope-filtered `FACTOR_EXPOSURE` resolve (`:198-200`) **and** a tenant-global `COVARIANCE` resolve (`:204`); build-args re-pinning (`:179-180`); a synthesized SYSTEM actor (`:212`); and the two VaR-named `resolved_*` columns (`:226-227`).

Enforcement asymmetry: `SCHEDULABLE_RUN_TYPES` checked **twice** (`:272` create, `:192` dispatch); `CADENCE_KINDS` **once** (`:274` create only — no DB CHECK, not re-checked at dispatch or update); `SCHEDULED_RUN_OUTCOMES` **never enforced**.

**Blast radius, corrected:** `create_schedule` has **zero non-test callers** and no API router touches schedules — verified. That means no OpenAPI/entitlement/FE change **and** that nothing outside tests exercises any of this (see OD-G/OD-H — it is why the CI downgrade smoke is blind).

## Part 2 — Standards grounding

- **`QS-11` (`numerical_quant_standards.md:66`)** governs directly: holiday calendars referenced explicitly, rolling convention declared.
- **GIPS 2.A.23.b** (cited in full at RM-1 §2.3): *"As of the calendar month end **or the last business day of the month**"* — the downstream consumer accepts either.
- **The substrate gap, verified:** `Calendar`/`CalendarHoliday` (ENT-006) exist as vocabulary only (`reference/models.py:90`, `:106`); there is **no business-day logic anywhere** (grep: no `is_business_day`/`next_business`/rolling helper). Confirms SCH-1's OD-SCH-1-F.
- **Quantified:** **11 of 36 (30.6%)** calendar month-ends in 2025–2027 fall on a weekend (independently recomputed by the verifier). The last-weekday rule cuts failures to holiday collisions: **4 of 144 months (2.8%) over 2024–2035** — `2024-03-29`, **`2027-05-31` (Memorial Day)**, `2029-03-30`, `2032-05-31`. The next collision lands inside the platform's likely first year of real-data operation.

## Part 3 — Design decisions

### OD-SCH-2-A — `model_version_id` nullable + a **total-enumeration** CHECK, with the service and the FK guard both registry-driven
`run_exposure` is the model-less rollup — no `model_version_id` parameter, passes `None` deliberately (`exposure/service.py:301`); `lim_1_decision_record.md:78` recorded the collision. The column is a CTRL-003 inventory-before-use affordance, so it relaxes **only** under a per-family rule. Four corrections from the verifier, all folded:

- **The CHECK must be a TOTAL ENUMERATION, not an implication (V2-M1).** The implication form `(target_run_type <> 'VAR' OR model_version_id IS NOT NULL) AND (target_run_type <> 'EXPOSURE' OR model_version_id IS NULL)` **fails OPEN** for any family not enumerated — a future `ACTIVE_RISK` row satisfies both conjuncts with either value. Ship instead:
  `(target_run_type = 'VAR' AND model_version_id IS NOT NULL) OR (target_run_type = 'EXPOSURE' AND model_version_id IS NULL)`.
  This is exclusive, exhaustive and **fails closed**: admitting family 3 then *requires* a migration, which makes the DB a deliberate third gate rather than an accident. (The classic NULL trap — a CHECK passing when it evaluates NULL — does not bite here because both discriminators are NOT NULL; worth one sentence, no more.)
- **The relaxation is a net LOSS of enforcement at the unit tier (V2-B4).** SQLite builds its schema from ORM metadata (`conftest.py:33`) and this repo creates every CHECK imperatively in migrations — so today SQLite enforces NOT NULL, and after the relaxation it enforces **nothing**, while every scheduler/limit/breach/notification unit test runs there. So `_validate_config` must carry the rule **in both directions** (required for VAR, *forbidden* for EXPOSURE), registry-driven, or the service admits on SQLite what PG rejects at flush with a raw `IntegrityError`.
- **The CAD-1 FK guard is called UNCONDITIONALLY (V2-B5, verified `service.py:315-317`)** — `assert_model_version_in_tenant` with `None` stringifies to `"None"`, matches nothing, and raises, so **an EXPOSURE schedule cannot be created at all** until this is touched. The draft never listed it. **And the obvious fix is a trap:** `if model_version_id: assert_...` gates a CTRL-003 control on the *truthiness of caller input*, so a VAR schedule created with `None` skips the guard entirely — the LIM-1 fail-open lesson exactly. Gate on the **registry declaration**, never the value, with `_validate_config` having already refused a falsy value for a requiring family. Negative control: a VAR schedule with `model_version_id=None` is refused **by the model-version message**, not by a downstream FK.
- **The conformance pin must be BEHAVIORAL, not textual (V2-M2).** `pg_get_constraintdef()` returns a normalized expression (casts inserted, IN-lists rewritten — `0041:44-46` pinned exactly this), so a text comparison is either brittle or vacuous (`assert "VAR" in defn` passes for any expression naming the family). The draft's "pin the CHECK expression" framing is withdrawn. Ship instead a **matrix derived from the registry**, raw-inserting (never via `create_schedule`, or the service gate short-circuits it) each `(family × supply/omit)` combination and asserting accept/reject — with the **executed negative control** of dropping the constraint inside a savepoint and proving the same helper then fails. PG-only by construction; its value is contingent on the `ci.yml` wiring.

### OD-SCH-2-B — `interval_days` nullable + a per-cadence total CHECK; **`current_tick` must fail closed**
`(cadence_kind = 'INTERVAL' AND interval_days IS NOT NULL AND interval_days > 0) OR (cadence_kind = 'CALENDAR_MONTH_END' AND interval_days IS NULL)` — folding in the positivity check the DB does not have today. `_validate_config`'s `if interval_days <= 0` (`:278`) raises a raw `TypeError` on `None`; the draft fixed that site only. **The more dangerous site is `current_tick:85`**, which the draft missed (V1-B3): it runs on the **poll path**, and `select_active_due` is evaluated in the `for` **header** at `irp_worker/scheduler.py:76` — *outside* the per-schedule SAVEPOINT opened at `:77` (verified). So a raise there escapes `poll_tenant_schedules`, escapes `run_operational_tick_for_tenant`, and is caught only by the supervisor's per-tenant catch-all — **aborting all four phases** (schedules, breaches, deadlines, notifications) for that tenant every cycle, with no durable evidence. Reachable by a rollback (0053 applied, a month-end schedule created, image reverted). Therefore: `current_tick` fails closed with `ScheduleError` on an unresolvable cadence, **and** the per-schedule tick computation moves inside the isolation (skip-and-report, not raise), **and** `cadence_kind` gets its own DB CHECK so an unresolvable row cannot exist.

### OD-SCH-2-C — The grid: `CALENDAR_MONTH_END`, last **weekday**, `preceding` convention — with the start boundary and the tick instant both corrected
A new `CADENCE_CALENDAR_MONTH_END`. The dead `CADENCE_CALENDAR_RESERVED = "CALENDAR"` (`events.py:31`) is **not** reused — its docstring reserves it for a *business-day* cadence, a different concept.

**The tick is the last weekday of the calendar month** — the `preceding` convention (QS-11's vocabulary) over a weekend-only predicate: no holiday data needed, satisfies GIPS 2.A.23.b, and cuts failures from 30.6% to 2.8% (§2). The holiday residual is a recorded first-class limitation with its dates named; full ENT-006 resolution rides Wave-14 where the reference data arrives.

**THE START BOUNDARY IS A TICK PREDICATE (both lanes, independently — CONFIRMED per the ≥2-finder rule; the draft asserted the opposite).** The draft claimed `anchor_date`'s two roles "genuinely separate". They do not. All three gates compare **`now`** to the anchor (`service.py:102`, `:117`, `:149` — verified), never the **tick**; under INTERVAL the invariant `tick ≥ anchor` was held **structurally by the `if k < 0: k = 0` clamp** (`:91-92`), and a calendar grid has no clamp analogue — so the draft deleted the start-boundary control without noticing. Reproduced: an anchor of `2026-01-05` polled the next day computes tick `2025-12-31`, **35 days before the anchor**; across 2026, **341 of 365 anchors (93%) fire a pre-anchor tick on first poll**. Under OD-E that mints a governed EXPOSURE run **dated before the book was configured** — manufactured history feeding a governed number. Fix: add `if tick < _anchor_dt(schedule.anchor_date)` at all three sites (INTERVAL unchanged), with a mid-month-anchor test — the case `_mk` (`test_scheduler.py:64-79`) never generates.

**THE TICK INSTANT IS END-OF-DAY (V1-B2/V2-M8; the draft was silent).** `_anchor_dt` builds UTC midnight, so the draft's natural reading was `last-weekday @ 00:00Z` — the **start** of the month-end day. Under OD-E that is a bitemporal cutoff, not a label: it reaches `attach_marks_as_of(valid_at=tick)` → **`Valuation.valid_from <= valid_at`** (`valuation/valuation.py:363`, verified). An end-of-day mark for `T` is captured during/after `T`, so `valid_from > T 00:00Z` and **the mark is invisible** — the snapshot still builds from positions alone and the completeness gate FAILS the run, **every month**. It would also pair start-of-`T` positions with `T`-dated marks (a mixed-timing NAV) and value one business day early. So the tick is the **end** of the last weekday (`T 23:59:59.999999Z`): `as_of_valuation_date = T` still (RM-1 alignment holds), `valid_from <= tick` holds for same-day capture, and trades booked on `T` are included.
**Why the demo cannot catch this — design the fixture against it:** `campaign.py:435` seeds every valuation with `valid_from=_T0 = 2024-06-01` (verified), so `valid_from <= valid_at` is *unconditionally* true and any tick instant passes. SCH-2's test must seed a mark whose `valid_from` is a **capture time on `T`**.

**Held under attack (evidence):** the inverse is exact, monotone, unambiguous (15-min sweep across 2025–2027: `tick ≤ now` always, 37 half-open buckets, two polls in a bucket always agree) — **no double-fire arises from the grid function itself**; and `now` is frozen once per tick (`irp_worker/scheduler.py:168`) so all gates agree across a boundary. Also normalize `now` to UTC in `current_tick` — offset-invariance was verified across offsets −12…+14 but is currently accidental and undeclared.

### OD-SCH-2-D — The family dispatch registry, and where it must live
Entries declare what the generic dispatcher cannot infer: `produced_run_type`, `requires_model_version`, a per-family `dispatch(...)` callable, and — added by the verifier (V2-M6) — **`produces_run_on_failure`**, because the two families fail differently: VaR can refuse **pre-create** (`service.py:198-207`, yielding `calculation_run_id = NULL`), whereas EXPOSURE has **no upstream to resolve and therefore no pre-create gate** — its dominant failure (a missing mark) is a **post-create FAILED run**, leaving a committed `calculation_run` + audit events + a DQ result + a **non-NULL** `calculation_run_id` on the ledger row. So OD-C's holiday-residual disclosure is not a no-op: each bad month-end leaves durable governed artefacts.

**Placement is an import hazard (V2-M5).** The registry in `events.py` would force module-scope imports of the risk **and** exposure compute stacks into what is today a leaf vocabulary module that `irp_worker` imports for three string constants — a layering regression; the registry in `service.py` with `SCHEDULABLE_RUN_TYPES` still in `events.py` is a **circular import**. Only viable shape: registry **and** the derived schedulable set both in `scheduling/service.py`, `events.py` keeping the `TARGET_RUN_TYPE_*` literals. That is a public-name move (verified safe — no external readers) and `models.py:66`'s docstring reference must be re-pointed.

**Preserve the monkeypatch seam.** `test_scheduler_dispatch.py:243` patches the module global `svc.run_var`. A module-level `_dispatch_var` calling `run_var(...)` resolves through `__globals__` and the patch takes effect; a registry capturing the function object at import does not. **Verifier-held nuance:** every wrong implementation breaks *loudly* (the shipped test's `FAILED` assertion fails, or `monkeypatch.setattr` raises `AttributeError`), so the real constraint is that the registry must not move out of `service.py` without the test moving with it.

### OD-SCH-2-E — EXPOSURE's `as_of_valid_at` from the tick, declared per-family, with the intra-day convention explicit
No `Schedule` column carries an as-of; the tick is the only instant. SCH-2 does **not** make "tick = as-of" structural — it is a declaration inside the EXPOSURE registry entry, so the coupling is visible and reviewable. Per OD-C the instant is **end-of-day**, which is the economically correct month-end close. `base_currency` resolves from the portfolio as today. **`as_of_known_at` is pinned to the tick, not left to default (V1-M4):** `build_snapshot` would otherwise default it to `utcnow()`, making the governed month-end value a function of *when the worker happened to be up* — two fires of the same tick, minutes vs weeks apart, would differ whenever an input was corrected between. Pinning it makes the fire reproducible from the tick alone; the cost (ignoring later corrections) is the correct trade for a governed input and is recorded.

### OD-SCH-2-F — Two gates, not one; and the fence claim corrected
The registry is the single source for the **family** gate, collapsing the two `SCHEDULABLE_RUN_TYPES` checks to one derived set (verified safe: no other reader anywhere). **The cadence gate is orthogonal and is NOT registry-derivable (V2-M5)** — the draft conflated them. Its answer is OD-B's: fail closed in `current_tick` plus a DB CHECK.

**The fence claim was wrong (V2-M4).** The draft said `scheduling` alone has violated `risk`'s "nothing imports me" claim. The true importer list: **`risk`** is imported by `scheduling/service.py`, **`limit/service.py:61,62,73`**, and **`snapshot/service.py` at ~13 sites** (incl. `:1853`/`:3449` importing `risk.scenario`, a *service* module); **`exposure`** is imported by **`risk/factor_service.py:75`** and `snapshot/service.py:600,616`. So a test whitelisting only `scheduling` **fails on day one**. The honest fold is a **narrow but true** sweep whitelisting the real importers, which then only catches a *new* importer from the untouched packages — and since the existing sweeps whitelist by directory, each entry blanket-exempts a package. That dilution is the cost and is stated rather than glossed.

### OD-SCH-2-G — Migration `0053`, and a downgrade the draft got wrong twice
Two `alter_column(nullable=True)` + three `create_check_constraint` (family, cadence, and `cadence_kind` itself) — the `0042:44-61` precedent. ORM annotations become `Mapped[str | None]`/`Mapped[int | None]` or `alembic check` flags nullability drift. Identifier lengths are fine (longest ≈36 chars).

**The downgrade is a TWO-TABLE cascade (V2-B2 — the draft's claim was false).** The draft said "`schedule` has no append-only trigger, so the sandwich needs only the RLS toggle, not the `DISABLE TRIGGER` leg". True of `schedule` itself, **false as a conclusion**: `fk_scheduled_run_schedule_id_schedule` has **no `ON DELETE` clause** (verified `0049:113-117`) → `NO ACTION`, so deleting a schedule that has ever fired aborts with 23503; and the children live on `scheduled_run`, which has **both** FORCE RLS **and** the P0001 append-only trigger. So the body must delete children first, needing `DISABLE TRIGGER scheduled_run_append_only` **and** RLS toggles on **both** tables — precisely `0042`'s sandwich applied to the table the draft said it need not touch. The DELETE predicate is `model_version_id IS NULL **OR** interval_days IS NULL` (the relaxations are independent).
**Undisclosed consequence, now disclosed:** this destroys **IA append-only governed ledger evidence** (`scheduled_run`, ENT-062) and orphans the `calculation_run` rows those ticks produced of their tick provenance. `0042:16-19` and `0041:56-58` both disclose their destructive side-effects explicitly; SCH-2 must too — and OQ-SCH-2-7 asks whether the downgrade should delete at all rather than refuse.

**CI cannot exercise any of this today (V2-B3 — the draft's coverage claim was false).** The draft said "the CI downgrade smoke runs after the demo stages, so this is exercised against seeded rows". Verified false: **zero demo stages create a schedule** (`create_schedule` has no non-test caller at all), and `test_scheduler_pg.py` never *commits* a `scheduled_run` (both inserts are rolled back). So at `alembic downgrade base` the 0053 DELETE matches **zero rows**, the FK cascade is never hit, and the smoke goes green against a body that would fail on any real deployment. **Fix (mandatory, and it is why the slice is M/L):** a dedicated `test_scheduler_downgrade_pg.py` under a non-superuser owner-via-membership role — the ES-HS-1/DS-2 pattern already wired at `ci.yml:296`/`:350` — seeding an EXPOSURE + month-end schedule **with a committed `scheduled_run` child**, executing the downgrade body, asserting both tables clear and the re-tighten succeeds, then rolling back.

### OD-SCH-2-H — Tests, the demo stage, and the vocabulary fork
- **Nothing pins VaR-only** (no test asserts the frozenset; `test_scheduler.py:195` uses `ACTIVE_RISK`, which stays out) and **`cadence_kind` has zero coverage today** — the new cadence ships with the first tests that vocabulary has ever had.
- **A demo stage is mandatory, not optional (V2-M9).** Because `create_schedule` has no non-test caller, nothing outside unit tests would exercise the month-end grid, the EXPOSURE family, the registry, or the new CHECKs — the OPS-1 standing lesson verbatim ("a demo that cannot REACH a control does not demonstrate it"). A stage creating a month-end EXPOSURE schedule and driving one tick is **the highest-leverage single addition in the slice**: it proves the grid end-to-end, proves the registry outside the unit tier, **and** leaves committed unrepresentable rows so the downgrade smoke finally has something to delete (closing V2-B3's other half).
- Added: the month-end inverse + concurrent-poll bucket agreement; the mid-month-anchor start-boundary test; the same-day `valid_from` fixture (OD-C); the behavioral CHECK matrix + its negative control (OD-A); the three-way schedulable agreement; an EXPOSURE end-to-end fire asserting `resolved_*` NULL and `run.run_type == "EXPOSURE_AGGREGATE"`; the downgrade-body suite (OD-G). All new PG suites need `ci.yml` wiring — `_EXEMPT` is empty by design.
- **`test_limit_breach.py:166-177`** creates a VAR schedule, so a `create_schedule` signature change touches the LIM-1 suite; keeping both relaxed params as keywords with `None` defaults keeps all seven call sites compiling.
- **A vocabulary collision the draft missed (V2-M3).** `target_run_type` is **also** a column on `limit_definition` and `breach` (`limit/models.py:68`, `:113`; migration `0050`), where it holds **actual `calculation_run.run_type` values** (`_METRIC_MAP` keys on `RUN_TYPE_VAR`/`RUN_TYPE_ACTIVE_RISK`) — and it is **outward-facing**, on three generated DTOs and rendered by the OPS-1 UI. Minting `"EXPOSURE"` on the schedule side creates two divergent vocabularies behind one column name across two entities. **The cheaper alternative the draft never considered: make the schedule's key `"EXPOSURE_AGGREGATE"`** — the run_type itself; 18 chars fits `String(100)`, no new vocabulary, no divergence, and the CHECKs enumerate real run_types. Cost: the registry loses a hypothetical "one family, several run types" generality. This is OQ-SCH-2-8.

## Part 4a — Ratified outcomes (2026-07-27)
